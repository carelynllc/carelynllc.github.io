A Caregiver's Checklist for Doctor Appointments

September 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Doctor visits for an aging parent are short, and they move fast. If you walk in unprepared, it's easy to leave realizing you forgot to mention the thing that's been worrying you all week. A little preparation turns a rushed fifteen minutes into a visit that actually moves things forward.

Bring a current medication list

This is the single most useful thing you can carry in. Providers routinely ask for a complete list of everything your parent is taking — names, doses, and timing. Trying to recall it from memory is stressful and error-prone. A shared, always-current list means you can hand it over (or read it off) without hesitation.

Write down your questions ahead of time

In the moment, questions evaporate. Jot them down in the days before the visit, as they come up, and bring the list. Prioritize — lead with the one or two things that matter most, in case time runs short.

Track the changes since last time

Doctors make better decisions with context. Note what's changed since the last appointment: a new symptom, a rough stretch of sleep, a change in appetite or mood, a fall, a medication that seemed to cause a reaction. A care journal you and other family members have been keeping is gold here — it turns "I think it started a couple weeks ago?" into a real timeline.

Tip: If you can't attend in person, ask whether a family member can join by phone, and make sure whoever goes has access to the same medication list and notes so the information doesn't depend on one person's memory.

Capture the plan before you leave

Write down what was decided — new medications, dose changes, follow-up tests, the next appointment — before you walk out the door. Then share it with the rest of the care team so everyone's working from the same instructions.
